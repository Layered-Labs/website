Mass-casualty triage ranks patients by how badly they are hurt and how soon they need care. Nothing else should matter: not who the patient is, what they do, or how much schooling they have. As language models are tried for clinical decision support, including triage, it is worth checking that their decisions follow the same rule.

Earlier work has shown that language models can change their decisions when given demographic details that have nothing to do with the task. In medicine that is a fairness problem: two patients with the same injuries should get the same decision.

We ask a narrow question. If the only thing that changes is one sentence about a patient's education, does a model change its triage?
