Neither model's triage was invariant to education, even though no clinical detail changed and decoding was deterministic, so the differences are not random noise.

The two models erred in opposite directions. <ModelPill model="qwen" label="Qwen-2.5-72B" /> moved toward undertriage, which in a mass-casualty event can delay care for someone badly hurt. <ModelPill model="openai" label="GPT-4o-mini" /> moved toward overtriage, which uses up scarce resources faster. Bias in triage is a matter of direction as well as size.

One possible explanation is that an education cue brings in learned associations about status or competence that then affect how urgent a case seems. That is a hypothesis; this study does not test the mechanism.

The broader lesson is about evaluation. Accuracy and F1 were stable while individual decisions changed. Testing a model for clinical use needs to include whether its decisions stay the same for patients who are the same in every way that matters.
