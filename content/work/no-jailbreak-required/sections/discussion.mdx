Per-model refusal rates understate real-world risk. A provider can reasonably report that its model refuses 97 to 99% of harmful requests, and every provider can say the same, while the ecosystem as a whole still answers 8% of them. The relevant number for safety is not any one model's rate, but the rate at which some model complies.

Two things follow. First, providers would benefit from coordinating on safety: sharing the requests their models refuse, so a refusal by one is not undone by another. Second, refusal benchmarks should report ecosystem-level results alongside per-model ones: for a set of models, how many harmful requests does at least one of them answer?

That no request was answered by every model is encouraging. It shows the providers already agree on a core of harms; the work is extending that agreement to the edges.
