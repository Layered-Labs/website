The most accurate AI models on medical exam questions are reasoning models: <ModelPill model="openai" label="ChatGPT" />, <ModelPill model="claude" label="Claude" /> and <ModelPill model="gemini" label="Gemini" /> write out their reasoning before they answer. That makes them slow and expensive. A typical answer takes 3 to 6 seconds, and every question pays for the reasoning that comes before the answer.

System One models are a new kind of AI that skips that step. <ModelPill model="jev" label="Jev" /> from TypeSafe, and <ModelPill model="liquid" label="Liquid d1-ultra" /> and <ModelPill model="liquid" label="Liquid d1.1" /> from Liquid AI, read a question and return an answer straight away, along with how sure they are. TypeSafe released <ModelPill model="jev" label="Jev" /> in September 2026. These models are very fast and very cheap, but on their own they are less accurate than the large reasoning models.

That tradeoff only matters if every question has to go to one model or the other. If a fast model's confidence can be trusted, it can answer the questions it is sure about and pass the rest to a reasoning model. The question this work asks is whether a System One model's own confidence is good enough to decide which orthopaedic exam questions need an expensive reasoning model, and which do not.

Orthopaedics gives a clean test: MedMCQA contains 3,019 orthopaedics questions with a known correct answer, enough to measure how often a fast model is right at each level of confidence.
